Arrow marks sans

A subset of Figtree carrying U+2190 (←) and U+2192 (→), and nothing
else. 2 code points, 788 bytes at 400 and 792 at 700.

Figtree is licensed under the SIL Open Font License, Version 1.1:
https://scripts.sil.org/OFL — Copyright 2022 The Figtree Project
Authors (https://github.com/erikdkennedy/figtree). The copyright
declares no Reserved Font Name, so subsetting and redistribution are
permitted. The family has been renamed from "Figtree" to "Arrow marks
sans" inside the font, because this is a modified subset and must not
be mistaken for Figtree entire. Instanced from the variable file at
wght 400 and 700, which are the two weights the site's chrome draws.

Why it exists, and it is the same fault as the two patches beside it
in one more place. DM Sans sets this site's chrome, and every arrow on
the site lives in the chrome: "Browse the works →", "Read →", "← All
works", "All of <category> →". Read off the served file's cmap, DM
Sans holds U+2191 (↑) and U+2193 (↓) and does NOT hold U+2190 or
U+2192 — two of the four, which is the sort of thing only a cmap read
will tell you. So 71 → and 33 ← across the repository were drawn by
whatever sans the device happened to have: measured live with
getPlatformFontsForNode, 122 glyphs of the container's Inter.

What that looks like is a thin, pale arrow sitting beside heavy bold
letters, because a fallback face does not take DM Sans's weight with
it. Rendered side by side at the real 14px and 15px, the ↓ that DM
Sans does hold is plainly heavier than the → it does not.

Four faces were measured as candidates, by reading cmaps rather than
by reputation — Noto Sans, the source of `latin-marks-sans` beside
this, turns out not to hold the arrows either, so that patch could not
simply be extended. Figtree, IBM Plex Sans, Source Sans 3 and Work
Sans all have both at a weight axis. All four were cut, drawn beside
DM Sans at 400 and 700 and looked at: Figtree and Work Sans are
indistinguishable there, and Figtree is half the bytes. Both are
geometric sans faces, which is what DM Sans is.

Scoped by unicode-range in styles.css so it can only ever draw those
two marks; everything else is DM Sans's. The range declares exactly
what the files hold — 2 of 2, checked by reading the cmap back out of
the woff2 after writing it — because this repository's own lesson is
that a declaration which overstates what is inside is worse than no
declaration at all.

Note that `.category-arrow`'s › is deliberately NOT covered here. It
is a <button>, which does not inherit font-family, so it has always
drawn in the UA default; CLAUDE.md records that as intentional, and
naming it would change a glyph this had no business touching.
