Urdu dashes

A subset of Noto Sans carrying U+2013 (–) and U+2014 (—), and nothing
else. 2 code points, 980 bytes (948 before the rename).

Noto Sans is licensed under the SIL Open Font License, Version 1.1:
https://scripts.sil.org/OFL — the copyright carries no Reserved Font
Name, so subsetting and redistribution are permitted; this is the same
clearance `latin-marks-sans` beside it records. The family has been
renamed from "Noto Sans" to "Urdu dashes" inside the font, because this
is a modified subset and must not be mistaken for Noto Sans entire.

Why it exists, and this one is about WEIGHT ON THE WIRE rather than
about the shape. CLAUDE.md records looking at a patched em-dash against
an unpatched one and deciding it was not worth a file, because a dash
is a horizontal bar in every serif and nothing visible changed. That
judgement about the shape was right and still is.

What it did not weigh is what the missing glyph COSTS. Mehr holds
neither dash (211 code points, checked). The fallback behind it in
--font-urdu is Noto Nastaliq Urdu, so the browser really fetches Noto's
subset — 20,492 bytes — to draw a short horizontal line. Measured off
the wire, it is fetched on the homepage, the author page, the fatāwā
page and choice-of-words. That is exactly the "20KB over the wire to
draw two characters" that the `Urdu guillemets` rule was added to
prevent, in the one character the guillemets range does not cover.
There are 223 em-dashes across the repository, 64 in content.js alone.

Three candidate sources were cut and drawn in a real bio line at 19px
and looked at, because the thing that decides this is the side bearing
and no stack says what that is:

  Aslam      908 B   advance 1.000em, side bearing 0.000em
  Noto Sans  948 B   advance 1.000em, side bearing 0.040em
  Gentium  4,580 B   advance 0.778em, side bearing 0.028em

Aslam is the face CLAUDE.md had costed for this, and it is the wrong
one: with no side bearing at all its bar touches the glyphs on both
sides and reads tighter than the Noto Nastaliq it replaces. Noto Sans
has air on both sides and reads best of the three; Gentium is close but
five times the bytes, because its em-dash drags extra tables with it.

A file of its own rather than more code points on an existing face,
and that was forced by measurement too. Aslam is not fetched on the
author page; `latin-in-urdu` is not fetched on the homepage, the
fatāwā page or choice-of-words. There is no already-present file on
all four, so sharing one would have traded Noto's 20KB for a 53KB
Aslam fetch on the page with the most dashes on the site.

It carries NO size-adjust, for the reason `Urdu guillemets` gives: a
dash belongs to the Urdu clause around it, and at `Latin in Urdu`'s 78%
it would be a shorter bar than that clause wants.
