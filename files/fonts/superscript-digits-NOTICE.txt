Superscript digits

A thirteen-glyph subset carrying ⁰ ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶ ⁷ ⁸ ⁹ (U+2070-2079,
U+00B2-00B3, U+00B9) and nothing else. 1.4KB, one weight.

Cut from Noto Serif, licensed under the SIL Open Font License, Version
1.1: https://scripts.sil.org/OFL — no Reserved Font Name, so subsetting
and redistribution are permitted. Renamed inside the font from "Noto
Serif" to "Superscript digits", because this is a modified subset and
must not be mistaken for Noto Serif entire.

Built by merging two of Google's own subsets: Google splits ¹ ² ³ into
the `latin` file and ⁰ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶ ⁷ ⁸ ⁹ into `latin-ext`, so no single file
it serves holds all ten.

Why it exists: Gentium Book Plus has ¹ ² ³ ⁴ and does NOT have
⁰ ⁵ ⁶ ⁷ ⁸ ⁹. The site's posts number their references ¹ to ⁷, so in one
footnote list markers 1 to 4 were drawn by Gentium and 5, 6 and 7 by
whatever serif the reader's device supplied — twelve characters across
two posts, in the one place a reader compares two marks directly
against each other. Found by asking the browser which face painted each
character, not by reading the stack.

The ordering in styles.css is the whole point and is easy to get wrong.
This face is named BEFORE Gentium in --font-display, not after it. A
unicode-range does not grant priority: it only limits what a face may
draw. Put this one second and Gentium would keep ¹ ² ³ ⁴ and the list
would still be set in two faces — which is the fault, not a smaller
version of it.

One weight only. These are body-prose footnote markers, which are 400;
a marker inside a bold run gets the browser's synthesised bold, and at
the size a superscript is drawn that is not worth a second file.
