Latin in Urdu

A Latin subset of Gentium Book Plus — the face that already sets this
site's English — carrying A-Z, a-z, Latin-1 Supplement and Extended-A,
Latin Extended Additional (U+1E00-1E9F, the dotted and barred letters
the transliteration here needs), and the two guillemets « ». 406
codepoints, 16.9KB. Nothing else.

"Gentium" is SIL's Reserved Font Name, so the family has been renamed
inside the font to "Latin in Urdu" — which the OFL requires of a
modified copy, and which is right anyway: this is a cut of Gentium and
must not be mistaken for Gentium entire.

Built by merging Google's `latin` and `latin-ext` woff2 subsets of
Gentium Book Plus 400 and subsetting the result; neither of Google's
files holds the whole of what is wanted on its own.

Why it exists: an English term inside an Urdu sentence — `board of
directors`, `Shariah screening`, `companies/corporations`, and this
library is full of them — is drawn by none of the Urdu faces, because
Mehr withholds Latin letters and Noto Nastaliq has none. What stood
here before was a `local()` list naming DM Sans, Segoe UI, Roboto,
Helvetica and Arial, which is a claim about the reader's device rather
than a face: every reader got a different sans, and all of them got a
*sans*, inside a page whose English everywhere else is a serif.
Measured on the shares article: 330 glyphs drawn by Liberation Sans.

It also covers what Mehr simply does not have and nothing else was
catching: ī ā ū ḥ Ṭ ş ğ (29 uses on that one article) and the
guillemets around an Arabic quotation. Those two marks alone were
pulling a 20KB subset of Noto Nastaliq Urdu over the wire to draw two
characters — so self-hosting this file makes the page lighter, not
heavier: measured 363KB of fonts before, 360KB after.

400 only. Latin inside a bold Urdu run is 102 characters across the
whole site, which is not worth a second 17KB file; the browser
synthesises the weight, exactly as it already does for Mehr's own bold.

The ayn (ʿ) is not in here. Gentium has never had it, which is why
`Ayn and hamza` exists — that patch is named in --font-urdu behind
this one now, where it had only ever been named in --font-display.
