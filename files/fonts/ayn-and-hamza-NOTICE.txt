Ayn and hamza

A two-glyph subset of Cardo, carrying U+02BF (ʿ, ayn) and U+02BE
(ʾ, hamza) and nothing else.

The copyright notice carries no Reserved Font Name, so subsetting and
redistribution are permitted. The family has still been renamed from
"Cardo" to "Ayn and hamza" inside the font, because this is a modified
subset and must not be mistaken for Cardo entire.

Why it exists: Gentium Book Plus, which sets this site's English and
its Arabic transliteration, has no glyph for either mark in the build
Google Fonts serves — measured, not assumed: both Gentium families
carry only five glyphs from the whole U+02B0-02FF block and neither of
these two is among them. Without this file the ayn in "Sharīʿah" and
the hamza in "Qurʾān" fall through to whatever serif the reader's
device happens to have, which is the fault this change was made to fix.
The ayn alone appears 118 times across the site.

It is scoped by unicode-range in styles.css so it can only ever draw
those two marks. Everything else is Gentium's.
