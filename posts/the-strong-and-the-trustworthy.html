The Strong and the Trustworthy

21 September 2026

اردو میں پڑھیے

What Sūrah al-Qaṣaṣ teaches about hiring

﴿قالَت إِحداهُما يا أَبَتِ استَأجِرهُ إِنَّ خَيرَ مَنِ استَأجَرتَ القَوِيُّ الأَمينُ﴾

Surah Al-Qasas (28:26)

“One of the two women said: O my father, hire him. The best one you can hire is the strong and the trustworthy.”

Sooner or later, everyone who runs a shop, a school or even a household has to hand work over to someone else. Most of us make that choice on instinct, or on a friend’s word. The Qur’an gives us something better, and it comes from an unlikely place: a young woman speaking to her elderly father in Madyan.

At the well

Sayyidunā Mūsā (عليه السلام) reached the well of Madyan just as the shepherds were finishing. They had watered their flocks and dragged back over the mouth of the well a rock that normally needed ten men. He moved it on his own and drew water for two young women who had been standing off to one side with their sheep.¹

A little later one of the sisters came back, walking shyly, to say her father wanted to see him. On the way, he asked her to walk behind him and tell him which way to turn, because he would not walk behind a woman.² Her father, whom Imām al-Qurṭubī and Imām al-Nasafī (رحمهما الله) name as Sayyidunā Shuʿayb (عليه السلام), heard his story and put him at ease.³ Then his daughter said, hire him. She had seen how strong he was, and she had heard what he said to her on the road.²

She had seen it for herself

Notice that nothing in her advice depends on what he claimed about himself. She had two pieces of evidence: how he behaved at a crowded well, and how he behaved on an empty road where nobody would have known the difference. She trusted what she had seen, and her father trusted her.

Strength and trust together

Imām Ḥāfiẓ al-Dīn al-Nasafī (رحمه الله), the Ḥanafī mufassir, calls her words a sentence that gathers everything. If the person looking after your affairs has both competence and trustworthiness, he says, you can put the matter out of your mind, because it will be done as you wanted.⁴

Anyone who has employed people knows why. Skill without honesty keeps you awake at night. Honesty without skill leaves you doing the work yourself.

A gift for reading people

Sayyidunā ʿAbdullāh ibn Masʿūd (رضي الله عنه) once named the three people who judged character most sharply. The first was Sayyidunā Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq (رضي الله عنه), in choosing Sayyidunā ʿUmar al-Fārūq (رضي الله عنه) to follow him. The second was the daughter of Sayyidunā Shuʿayb (عليه السلام). The third was the ʿAzīz of Egypt, when he told his wife to treat Sayyidunā Yūsuf (عليه السلام) with honour.⁵

No shame in working for a wage

Imām al-Shāfiʿī (رحمه الله) took this verse as proof that Allah mentions hiring (ijārah) in His Book, and that some of His Prophets worked for wages themselves.⁶

Imām al-Qurṭubī (رحمه الله) goes further. Hiring was known and permitted among them, he says, and has been in every religion, because people simply cannot get by without it. Ordinary dealings between people depend on it.³

So the next time you have to take someone on, start with her father’s question: how do you know?
