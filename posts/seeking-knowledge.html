Seeking Knowledge: Three Sayings of the Early Scholars

25 November 2026

اردو میں پڑھیے

1. This world and the Hereafter both come through knowledge

Imām al-Rabīʿ ibn Sulaymān (رحمه الله) said that he heard Imām Muḥammad ibn Idrīs al-Shāfiʿī (رحمه الله) say:

مَنْ أَرَادَ الدُّنْيَا فَعَلَيْهِ بِالْعِلْمِ، وَمَنْ أَرَادَ الْآخِرَةَ فَعَلَيْهِ بِالْعِلْمِ

Whoever wants this world should hold fast to knowledge, and whoever wants the Hereafter should hold fast to knowledge.¹

2. Knowledge asks for the whole person

Imām al-Khaṭīb al-Baghdādī (رحمه الله) records this saying with his chain of narration:

الْعِلْمُ شَيْءٌ لَا يُعْطِيكَ بَعْضَهُ حَتَّى تُعْطِيَهُ كُلَّكَ، وَأَنْتَ إِذَا أَعْطَيْتَهُ كُلَّكَ، مِنْ إِعْطَائِهِ الْبَعْضَ عَلَى خَطَرٍ

Knowledge will not give you even a part of itself until you give it all of yourself. And even when you have given it everything, whether it gives you that part is still uncertain.²

3. When the seeking stops, the knowledge goes

Imām al-Ghazālī (رحمه الله) quotes ʿAbdullāh ibn al-Mubārak (رحمه الله):

لَا يَزَالُ الْمَرْءُ عَالِمًا مَا طَلَبَ الْعِلْمَ، فَإِذَا ظَنَّ أَنَّهُ قَدْ عَلِمَ فَقَدْ جَهِلَ

A person remains a scholar as long as he keeps seeking knowledge. The moment he thinks he has learnt it all, he has become ignorant.³
